Before you trust a number

  • Analyse every cylinder after blending and again before diving. This tool predicts a mix; only an analyser measures one.
  • Oxygen decanting demands oxygen-clean, oxygen-compatible cylinders, valves and whips, and slow, controlled flow. Adiabatic compression at a fast-opening valve is what starts oxygen fires.
  • Blend only within your training and your fill station's operating procedures.
  • Never exceed the cylinder's working pressure, and never exceed it to compensate for heat.

How the numbers are worked out

  • Real gas. A virial equation of state (B, C and D coefficients for O₂, N₂ and He with cross terms) replaces PV=nRT. Every stop pressure is the pressure that the accumulated moles exert, not a sum of partial pressures. Good to roughly ±1% to 300 bar; ideal-gas mode is there for comparison.
  • Air is taken as 20.9% O₂ / 79.1% N₂, with argon counted as nitrogen. With no nitrogen bank, all of the nitrogen in a trimix arrives as air, so the oxygen cannot go below 20.9% of the non-helium part of the mix: 9.4% at 55% helium, 6.3% at 70%. The oxygen control stops there.
  • Heat. A two-node model tracks the gas and the cylinder wall through the fill: enthalpy in from the compressor or bank, convection to the wall, and the wall losing heat to air or water. It is an estimate — treat the hot figures as guidance and confirm on the settled pressure.
